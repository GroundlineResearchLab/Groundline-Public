# Groundline-Public Briefs

**Project:** Groundline Research Lab

**Repository:** Groundline-Public

**Section:** Public Briefs

**Framework:** DGCP™

## Purpose

The `briefs/` directory contains concise public research briefs produced by Groundline Research Lab.

Briefs are designed to communicate a focused research finding, data issue, evidence pattern, methodological concern, or public data question without requiring a full article.

A brief should remain concise without weakening evidence discipline.

## Scope

Public briefs may address topics including:

• Data quality.
• Data provenance.
• Source reliability.
• Data access.
• Data classification.
• Research literacy.
• Evidence interpretation.
• Data lifecycle.
• Data infrastructure.
• Verification methods.
• Dataset limitations.
• Information integrity.
• Public understanding of data systems.

## Brief Function

A public brief should answer a narrow question or explain a clearly defined issue.

It may summarize:

1. What was observed.
2. What evidence supports the observation.
3. What the evidence does not establish.
4. Why the issue matters.
5. What readers should understand about the data or research process.

A brief should not attempt to compress an entire research programme into a short publication.

## Evidence Standard

Every brief should remain within the scope of the evidence examined.

A brief must not transform:

• Preliminary evidence into final findings.
• Limited coverage into system wide conclusions.
• Announced activity into completed execution.
• Missing data into zero.
• Correlation into causation.
• Source claims into independently verified facts without verification.

Important limitations should remain visible.

## Provenance

Where relevant, briefs should identify:

• Primary source.
• Publishing institution.
• Publication date.
• Retrieval date.
• Dataset or document identifier.
• Relevant record or version.
• Supporting public references.

A concise publication may use a shorter source presentation, but provenance must remain sufficient for verification.

## Brief Structure

A typical brief may use the following structure:

```text
# Title

Publication information

## Question

## What the Evidence Shows

## Why It Matters

## Limitations

## Sources
```

The structure may be adapted to the subject.

## Public Readability

Briefs should use clear language and avoid unnecessary technical complexity.

Simplification must not remove:

• Material uncertainty.
• Evidence limitations.
• Source context.
• Important definitions.
• Relevant qualifications.
• Distinctions between observation and interpretation.

## Interpretation

Where interpretation is included, it should be identifiable as Groundline Research Lab interpretation rather than source evidence.

Interpretation must remain proportionate to the available evidence.

## Corrections and Updates

Material corrections should remain traceable.

Where new evidence changes the central finding of a brief, a corrected, updated, or superseding record may be published.

Historical public records should not be silently rewritten when the substantive meaning changes.

## Publication Boundary

Only briefs approved for public release belong in this directory.

Internal notes, working hypotheses, incomplete source reviews, private research records, and restricted material should remain outside `Groundline-Public`.

## Organization

Briefs may be organized by year and month.

Example:

```text
briefs/
├── README.md
└── 2026/
    └── 09/
```

Additional structure should be introduced only when needed.
