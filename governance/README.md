# Groundline-Public Governance

**Project:** Groundline Research Lab

**Repository:** Groundline-Public

**Framework:** DGCP™

**Governance Status:** Active

**License Reference:** DGCP | MMFARM-POL-2025

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the governance rules for material published through `Groundline-Public`.

`Groundline-Public` is the public publication layer of Groundline Research Lab.

Its purpose is to make selected research and educational material publicly accessible while preserving evidence quality, provenance, traceability, research status, and publication boundaries.

## 2. Public Repository Boundary

Material in this repository is public by design.

Only content approved for public release should be committed.

The repository must not be used as a temporary workspace for:

• Unfinished research.
• Internal notes.
• Private synthesis.
• Restricted evidence.
• Internal source collections.
• Unresolved research logic.
• Confidential information.
• Material awaiting classification.

Public availability is a publication state, not merely a repository location.

## 3. Publication Standard

Before a record is published, it should be checked for:

1. Evidence quality.
2. Provenance.
3. Source identity.
4. Research status.
5. Classification.
6. Unsupported claims.
7. Rights and licensing restrictions.
8. Sensitive or restricted information.
9. Public readability.
10. Traceability to supporting material where appropriate.

A record should not be treated as publication ready while a material evidence or classification issue remains unresolved.

## 4. Evidence First

Groundline-Public follows an evidence first standard.

Evidence should be separated from interpretation.

A publication should not state more than the available evidence supports.

Where evidence is incomplete, conditional, disputed, preliminary, or limited in scope, the public record should preserve that limitation.

Authority of source and strength of evidence are separate questions.

## 5. Provenance

Public records should preserve enough provenance to support independent examination.

Where relevant, records should identify:

• Source organization or author.
• Source title.
• Publication date.
• Retrieval date.
• Document identifier.
• Dataset identifier.
• Record identifier.
• Version.
• Archive location.
• Transformation or calculation applied to source material.

Provenance should not be removed solely to simplify presentation where its removal would materially reduce verifiability.

## 6. Source Hierarchy

Where practical, Groundline-Public should prefer the most direct and authoritative source available for the claim being made.

Source categories may include:

1. Primary records.
2. Official publications.
3. Original datasets.
4. Research papers.
5. Institutional analysis.
6. Reputable secondary reporting.
7. Commentary or tertiary material.

A secondary source must not be described as a primary source.

A source hierarchy does not automatically determine truth.

Each source must still be assessed within its scope and context.

## 7. Research State

Public records should distinguish among research states where relevant.

### Observation

What was directly recorded, measured, published, or found.

### Evidence

The source material supporting or constraining the observation.

### Structure

Relationships, classifications, processes, or system connections identified from the evidence.

### Interpretation

What the evidence may mean within the defined research scope.

### Hypothesis

A proposition that requires further testing or evidence.

### Conclusion

A finding supported by the available evidence within the stated scope.

These states should not be presented as interchangeable.

## 8. Claims and Qualification

Claims must remain proportionate to the evidence.

Groundline-Public should avoid:

• Unsupported certainty.
• False precision.
• Causal claims based only on correlation.
• Generalization beyond the examined evidence.
• Treating plans as completed outcomes.
• Treating announcements as execution.
• Treating access as use.
• Treating availability as adoption.
• Treating absence of evidence as evidence of absence.

Where uncertainty matters, it should be stated.

## 9. Public Simplification

Public communication may simplify technical language and structure.

Simplification must not remove material facts, qualifications, dependencies, limitations, or uncertainty.

Readable does not mean less rigorous.

Educational presentation should preserve the logic necessary for readers to distinguish evidence from interpretation.

## 10. Data Publication

Data published through this repository should include sufficient context to understand:

1. What the data represents.
2. Where it came from.
3. When it was collected or retrieved.
4. How it was transformed.
5. What fields or units mean.
6. Known limitations.
7. Missing values or exclusions where relevant.
8. Applicable source or licensing restrictions.

A dataset should not be published without enough context to interpret it responsibly.

## 11. External Data

Public availability of external data does not automatically grant unrestricted reuse rights.

Before publishing copied, transformed, or redistributed external data, source rights and applicable terms should be checked.

Where redistribution is not appropriate, Groundline-Public should publish references, derived findings, or reproducible methods instead of reproducing restricted source material.

## 12. Corrections

Material errors should be corrected transparently.

A correction should preserve, where relevant:

1. The affected record.
2. The original publication state.
3. The corrected information.
4. The reason for correction.
5. The effective date.
6. The relationship between the original and corrected record.

Historical records should not be silently rewritten when the correction materially changes the evidentiary or analytical meaning.

## 13. Updates and Superseding Records

New evidence may change a research finding.

Where a later publication materially updates an earlier publication, the relationship should be explicit.

Possible states include:

• Updated.
• Corrected.
• Superseded.
• Expanded.
• Methodology revised.
• Evidence added.

Superseding a record does not require erasing the historical record.

## 14. Version and Record Integrity

Canonical public records should remain identifiable.

Material changes should preserve sufficient version or record information to determine what was publicly available at a given time.

Externally modified copies are not canonical Groundline-Public records.

## 15. Publication Classification

Public outputs may include:

• Articles.
• Briefs.
• Explainers.
• Public research records.
• Public datasets.
• Data documentation.
• Methodology notes.
• Evidence indexes.
• Educational material.
• Public synthesis.

Classification should describe what the record is and how it should be interpreted.

## 16. Sensitive Information

Public records must be checked for information that should not be released.

This may include:

• Personal information.
• Credentials or authentication information.
• Confidential material.
• Restricted source material.
• Security sensitive operational details.
• Legally protected information.
• Information prohibited from redistribution.

Evidence value does not override lawful or legitimate disclosure restrictions.

## 17. Publication Rights

Publication requires consideration of both research value and publication rights.

A source may be valid evidence while still being unsuitable for direct republication.

Citation, quotation, transformation, redistribution, and dataset publication should be treated as separate rights questions.

## 18. Relationship to Groundline Research Lab

Groundline Research Lab is the research environment.

`Groundline-Public` is the public output layer.

Internal research processes may include material that never enters this repository.

Public records should contain only the information necessary for the intended public research or educational purpose while preserving sufficient traceability.

## 19. Relationship to DGCP™

Groundline Research Lab operates under the DGCP™ Framework.

DGCP™ provides the broader governance principles for evidence integrity, continuity, proof, traceability, and system structure.

`Groundline-Public` applies those principles specifically to public research and educational publication.

## 21. Governance Changes

Governance changes apply forward from their effective state unless explicitly documented otherwise.

New rules should not silently change the historical governance status of previously published records.

Material governance changes should preserve a clear record of what changed and when.
