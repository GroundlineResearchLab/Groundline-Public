# Groundline-Public Articles

**Project:** Groundline Research Lab

**Repository:** Groundline-Public

**Section:** Public Articles

**Framework:** DGCP™

## Purpose

The `articles/` directory contains public articles produced by Groundline Research Lab.

Articles translate research, evidence, and data related findings into accessible public explanations while preserving provenance, qualification, and research integrity.

An article is a public communication layer.

It must not present interpretation as evidence or remove material uncertainty for the sake of readability.

## Scope

Articles may cover topics including:

• Data systems.
• Data provenance.
• Data quality.
• Research literacy.
• Evidence quality.
• Data lifecycle.
• Source evaluation.
• Verification.
• Data governance.
• Data access.
• Data classification.
• Cross border data.
• Industrial data.
• AI training data.
• National data infrastructure.
• Public understanding of research and information systems.

The scope may expand as Groundline Research Lab develops new research domains.

## Article Standard

Each article should clearly identify:

1. Title.
2. Publication date.
3. Research topic or domain.
4. Relevant sources.
5. Material evidence supporting the article.
6. Important limitations or qualifications.
7. Publication status where necessary.

Articles should remain understandable without requiring access to internal Groundline research material.

## Evidence Discipline

Articles must remain proportionate to the evidence available.

Public writing should not convert:

• Plans into completed outcomes.
• Announcements into implementation.
• Availability into actual use.
• Correlation into causation.
• Estimates into verified measurements.
• Partial data into complete system representation.
• Unverified claims into established facts.

Where these distinctions matter, they should remain visible in the article.

## Source Use

Sources should be selected according to relevance, authority, proximity to the original information, and suitability for the claim being made.

Primary and official sources should be preferred where materially relevant and available.

Secondary reporting may be used when appropriate but should not be represented as the original source.

## Public Readability

Articles may simplify terminology and technical structure for public understanding.

Simplification must not materially change:

• Evidence status.
• Source meaning.
• Research scope.
• Uncertainty.
• Limitations.
• Causal relationships.
• Research conclusions.

Accessible writing and evidence discipline should operate together.

## Article Structure

Article structure may vary according to subject.

A typical article may contain:

```text id="3v17aj"
# Title

Publication information

## Context

## What the Evidence Shows

## How the System Works

## Why It Matters

## Limits and Uncertainty

## Sources
```

This is a reference structure, not a mandatory template for every publication.

## Research and Interpretation

Where analytical interpretation is included, the distinction between source evidence and Groundline interpretation should remain clear.

Interpretation should answer what the evidence may mean within a defined scope.

It should not claim certainty beyond that scope.

## Corrections

Material errors should be corrected transparently.

Where a correction changes the meaning, evidence basis, or conclusion of an article, the correction should remain traceable to the earlier publication state.

Minor typographical corrections that do not change substantive meaning may be handled according to repository governance.

## Updates

New evidence may justify an updated or superseding article.

An updated article should not silently erase material historical differences.

Where relevant, the relationship between earlier and later publications should be documented.

## Publication Boundary

Only articles approved for public release belong in this directory.

Drafts, incomplete source reviews, private notes, internal analysis, unresolved hypotheses, and restricted research should remain outside `Groundline-Public`.

## Organization

Articles may be organized by publication year and month.

Example:

```text id="zc7paf"
articles/
├── README.md
└── 2026/
    └── 09/
```

Additional structure should be introduced only when publication volume requires it.
